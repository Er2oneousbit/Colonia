# Colonia

[![CI](https://github.com/Er2oneousbit/Caesar/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/Er2oneousbit/Caesar/actions/workflows/ci.yml)
[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-MIT-blue.svg)](LICENSE)

*Veni, vidi, aedificavi.*

A browser city builder in the spirit of **Caesar III**. Lay out roads, settle families, keep them fed, safe, faithful and entertained, and watch tents grow into palaces as walkers carry services through the streets. Trade with the empire by caravan and by ship, and raise legions, archers and cavalry to hold the walls when the raiders come.

Everything is original: the art is drawn in code, the sound and music are synthesized live, and all names and text are written for this game. Nothing from any commercial game is included. (If you want the *original* Caesar III, you need to own it; the open-source [Julius](https://github.com/bvschaik/julius) engine runs it on modern systems.)

---

## Play it

**[Play in your browser](https://er2oneousbit.github.io/Colonia/)**: Chrome, Edge or Firefox, on a computer or a phone.

Or download **[`dist/colonia.html`](dist/colonia.html)** (the *Download raw file* button on that page) and open it. It is the whole game in one file: no install, no account, nothing to set up. It works offline.

Saved games stay in your browser. To keep a backup or move a city to another computer, press 💾 beside any save in the Save or Load menu to download it as a file (or *Export current game* for the game you are playing).

## What's in it

* **Latin names:** every building goes by its Roman name, a Horreum for your goods, the Castra for your legion, with its English name beside it in the build menu and when you click it.
* **The housing ladder of the original:** 20 levels, from a tent to an imperial palace. Homes grow from single tiles into 2x2 insulae and villas, 3x3 villas and 4x4 palaces as you bring them water, food, gods, schools, baths, entertainment and fine goods. Click any home to see exactly what it needs next.
* **A campaign** of ten steps, from a riverside village to a great city of many districts (about half an hour for the first, a few hours for the last). From step 3 on you choose your next post: a peaceful province judged on what you build, or a military one with raiders and a legion to raise. Win the last and Rome hails you Caesar. And a **sandbox** with five landscapes, four map sizes (up to *Uber*, 256x256), your choice of raids and of six places on the empire map.
* **Crime:** unhappy homes send out protesters, thieves who rob the Forum or a market, and in a city at the end of its patience, rioters who burn their way toward its finest buildings. Prefects patrol as police and chase criminals down. The Crime overlay shows where trouble is brewing and why.
* **Disease:** crowded, unhealthy homes can fall sick, lose people and pass it next door. A Medicus sends physicians to cure them, and baths, barbers, fountains, food and a hospital keep it away. The Disease overlay shows which homes are at risk.
* **The five gods of the original:** Ceres, Neptune, Mercury, Mars and Venus. Keep them content with temples (a large temple counts as two) and festivals at their temples (a bigger feast needs more priests: a temple has one, a large temple two; a grand one needs an Oracle too), paid in food and, large or grand, wine, and they bless the city (a bumper harvest, a trade windfall, food for the granaries, peace, a happier people); neglect them (a god left more than a year without a festival grows cooler month by month) and they strike, and an angered Mercury or Venus strikes harder the second time.
* **Events, as in the original:** Rome raises or cuts the wage your workers expect, landslides or storms stop caravans or ships for 3 months, bad water lowers city health, a mine caves in or a clay pit floods. Some missions schedule an earthquake whose cracks spread through the city for days and leave rock where they pass, a new emperor (your favor starts afresh at 50), or a price change. Fewer on Easy, more on Hard and Insane; the sandbox setup has a switch for each, and one for them all.
* **Advisors (F2)** for every part of the city. The Health, Education and Entertainment advisors show, for each kind of building, how many work, whom they reach and how many of the people whose homes need them they serve, with one line of advice on what holds homes back most; the Overview gives city health and crime at a glance.
* **Fishing:** a shipyard builds fishing boats from timber; a wharf's boat sails out to where the gulls circle and brings the catch home. Fish is a food of its own, and the sea does not freeze in winter.
* **Native villages** in two late provinces (and the sandbox if you like): the Ligurians attack whatever you build on their land until a missionary from a mission post calms them; calmed, they come to buy your exports.
* **Two kinds of bridge**, as in the original: a stone ship bridge high enough for every boat to sail under, and a cheaper timber low bridge that no boat passes. A low bridge can cut a dock off from the sea (you are warned while you place it), and keeps raider ships out of the river above it.
* **Turn your buildings:** a building held beside one road turns its front to that road by itself; press **R** while placing one to turn it a quarter turn yourself (every building is drawn from all four sides), and your turn holds until you pick another tool; the next of its kind starts the same way. Buildings by the water still face it (and must stand right at its edge), and an arch still follows its road.
* **Turn the view:** see the city from any of its four sides (**Q**, or the buttons in the top bar), as in the original; the minimap turns with it.
* **Monuments:** from the sixth step of the campaign (and in the sandbox) a city may raise one great work: a Great Sanctuary to one god, the Pantheon, a Lighthouse, a Caravanserai, Great Baths or a Hall of Justice. A work camp's ox carts bring each stage's clay, timber, marble, iron and finer goods from your warehouses while its crew builds, stage by stage, as long as it is fed and watered. Finished, each changes the whole city: a god who never strikes and blesses sooner, more trade by sea or by land, baths for every home around, more taxes and less crime. Raiders set a site back, and on Insane they can raze it.
* **The Circus:** a 15-tile hippodrome with chariot races (a Factio sends the teams), laid east-west or, turned, north-south: the grandest show in the city and the key to its finest palaces. Pay for great races there, or for gladiator games at the Great Arena, to lift the whole city's mood for a few months.
* **Sea raids and a fleet** (new, not in the original, which had no war at sea): where a river or the coast reaches the sea, about a third of raids come by ship, throwing fire pots at boats and buildings by the shore before they land. A Navalia builds liburnians, light warships with two banks of oars and a bronze ram, from timber, iron and linen; naval stations (Stationes) berth them in squadrons you send out like a fort's soldiers, and the Portus, a training harbor, teaches their crews to row in time. A *Sea raids* switch turns it off.
* **Cloth and clothing** (new, not in the original): flax fields that flower blue, a Textrinum (linen weaver) at its loom and a Taberna Vestiaria (clothing maker) with tunics drying in the yard. From the Insula up, homes need clothing too; linen can also be bought from Hispania and Egypt.
* **Marble** (new, not in the original): the grand temples, the Oracle, the governor's palace, the Great Arena, the hippodrome and the bigger statues are built of marble from your warehouses as well as money, and the finest homes, from the Marble Villa up, want marble too. Quarry it beside the rocks or buy it from Greece.
* **Gardeners** (new, not in the original, whose decorations needed no upkeep): a Topiaria (gardeners' yard) sends gardeners down the streets to tend the gardens and statues beside them. Left untended for a month, a garden dries and a statue dulls, and their desirability fades over five months to a quarter, until the next gardener puts it back.
* **A Hall of Fame** (new, not in the original): every campaign win is scored on its ratings, its people, its pace and the difficulty, with a bonus for each distant battle won and raid repelled and for a finished monument; the ten best wins and your career's score are kept from game to game.
* **The governor's career:** a rank from Citizen up to Caesar, a salary you set yourself, up to your rank's (Rome thanks a modest governor), paid into personal savings that follow you from mission to mission, gifts to the Emperor from those savings, and a residence of your own: a house, a villa or a marble palace.
* **Four difficulties**, Easy to Insane.
* **Auto-pause** on the events you choose in *Settings* (a fire, a scouted raid, raiders arriving, Caesar's letters, a collapse, disease), with a note that takes you there; and **idle buildings** one click (or the **I** key) apart, or every building of a kind in turn from its panel.
* **Trade** by land and sea with twelve partner cities, each buying and selling its own goods up to a yearly quota (a busy route sends its traders more often, a far one less often) at its own prices: a far partner charges more and pays more, each province has a few goods cheap or dear, and every price moves a little each New Year. Choose whom you trade each good with: every partner's card has a switch per good, so you can sell only to the buyers who pay best. Ships come alongside your Emporium (trade dock), a stone quay out over the water like every harbor building, and wait there while its workers unload them and bring your exports aboard. An empire map (E) of the Mediterranean, with your province where its city stood, that shows the caravans and ships on their way, the warbands gathering against you (from the first traders' word six months out; your scouts report their size and road three months out, and a last warning comes a month before), Caesar's legions on the road and your troops marching to a distant battle.
* **Granary and warehouse orders:** per good, accept it, refuse it, or *get* it (the building's cart fetches it from other storage), and an *Empty* switch that sends everything elsewhere.
* **Defense:** a barracks, forts for legionaries, archers and cavalry (on horses you breed or import, kept at the ranch until a barracks needs them), watchtowers, walls and gates. Forts at rest hold their ground, as in the original, their men resting in the fort's yard and standing to outside its walls while enemies are about: deploy them to meet raiders in the field (a deployed fort takes no recruits until it is recalled). A Campus (military academy) trains your soldiers, new recruits on their way and the men already in a fort one at a time while it is at rest: trained legionaries holding their ground shrug off sling stones and arrows.
* **Enemies of their place and time:** each military province is raided by its own people, as in the original: Gaulish swordsmen and axemen, Ligurian hillmen and slingers after your food, Carthaginians with Numidian horse, hoplites and war elephants, Boii war chariots, Lusitanian raiders, the Cimbri and Teutones. Each people makes for its own targets and breaks at its own losses; the warnings, the empire map and the Military advisor name them.
* **Wolves** in the woods of the northern provinces (and a sandbox switch): packs that fall on cart pushers, traders and settlers who pass near, and grow back until your soldiers kill the last of them. And the **gladiators' revolt** in Urbs Magna and Puteoli, if a gladiator school is at work.
* **The Emperor's wrath and his wars:** let his favor sink to 10 and Caesar sends his own legions against your residence and finest homes, a year's march from Rome, with reminders halfway and a month out of what your favor would make them do; win back his favor before they arrive and they turn for home. He also calls for troops to save a city of the empire: send your forts (and, for a city by the sea, your fleet) in time and strong enough, and he grants you a triumphal arch to build across a road.
* **A living world:** day and night, four seasons with snow in winter, rain and thunderstorms, fluttering flags, busy markets, crowds at the shows, chariots racing round the spina.
* **A WebGL renderer (beta):** *Settings > Renderer* draws the same city with your graphics card, on lit 3D ground (meadows, scrub, forest floor, beaches, roads, water with depth and reflections; farms' fields that sprout, grow and ripen, yards, burned ruins; the seasons, snow and rain on it), the well, the fountain, the farms, the granary, the market, the forum, the warehouse, the prefecture, the engineer's post, the town walls and the watchtower and the fleet's three harbour buildings as 3D models (the fountain's look follows its neighbourhood, from a plain lava basin in a poor quarter to a marble fountain house among villas; a farm shows its season, its harvest and its herd; a granary shows how full it is, sacks, baskets, hams and fish piling up round it; the market's stalls lay out what it has to sell, fuller as its stock grows; the forum's doors stand open and coin lies on its counting table while it works; the warehouse's court shows how full it is and what it holds, load by load: amphorae of wine and oil, timber, marble, iron, sacks and baskets, cloth, arms; the prefecture is a brick watch house of the vigiles, its buckets, ladder and hooks gone from their racks while its men are out at a fire; the engineer's post is a builders' yard with a hoist, stone, bricks and timber and a surveyor's groma by the street; the town walls in the province's stone, polygonal blocks, squared tufa or limestone, or brick, with towers at their corners and along their runs, gates with arched passages between round towers that shut when an enemy comes near, cracks, fallen merlons and breaches where raiders strike; the watchtower a stone tower with a timber gallery, its archers on watch and a torch out of its window at night; the Navalia builds its liburnian on an open slip beside a ship shed, keel, planks and paint as the work goes on, the Statio's beacon burns on its tower at night, and at the Portus a new ship's crew pulls at the rowing benches on the shore while the boarding bridge drops onto a practice hulk), the countryside's trees and rocks in 3D too (cypress, stone pine, holm and downy oak, chestnut, plane, poplar and pollard willow by the rivers, wild olive, laurel, myrtle, wild cherry and almond in blossom, date palms at the oases; limestone outcrops and lava boulders), swaying in the wind, turning with the seasons and holding the snow, more to come, and closer zooms (3x, 4x and 6x) to see them up close; the Classic renderer stays the default.
* **Living people in 3D (WebGL renderer):** every building with people in it is peopled by moving, dressed Romans: a priest with his toga drawn over his head walks from the temple steps to the altar and pours from his dish over the fire, a boy holds the incense box, a flute player plays at a festival while the crowd in wreaths cheers and prays; boys write on their wax tablets and read their rolls while the master in his chair raises his hand; a clerk counts coin and a citizen pays from his purse; senators in the toga talk on the steps of the senate while the magistrate speaks at the door and his lictors wait with the fasces (soldiers with spears and shields come out when trouble nears); a fireman rocks the pump's beam; the librarian takes rolls down from the cupboards; the barber shaves his client; the governor's guests recline at dinner; a man turns the engineer's windlass; sentries pace the forts' walls, archers draw and loose, recruits strike at the post with sword and shield; a new ship's crew rows at the Portus's frame in time to the hortator's mallet. Tunics with their stripes, the toga, the stola and palla, soldiers' mail; weight shifting, breathing and looking about, never two in step unless they keep time together (a crew to its hortator's beat); snow settles on their shoulders in winter.
* **Walkers in 3D (WebGL renderer):** every walker of the city is a moving, dressed Roman: a vigil in red with his bucket (running to a fire, then throwing water at it), a priest with his toga over his head, a market woman with her basket on her head, a cart pusher leaning into a handcart loaded with what he carries, a farm wagon behind its ox, a trader leading his pack mules, a charioteer on his chariot, a family of newcomers with their bundles, a rioter with his torch. Their legs step with the ground they cover at any game speed and stop when you pause, and they turn at a corner as a walker does. A click on one, or on his cart, opens his panel.
* **Soldiers, raiders and wolves in 3D (WebGL renderer):** your legionaries march in step in bronze helmets with horsehair crests, mail and the curved scutum painted with the legion's wings and thunderbolts, behind a standard bearer in a bear's skin; they close ranks behind their shields when the enemy comes near, throw the pilum and fight with the gladius. Syrian archers in conical helmets draw and loose, troopers gallop on saddled horses. Each people raids in its own dress: Gauls with limed hair, moustaches, checked trousers and long swords, Ligurian hillmen with slings, Numidians with javelins riding bareback, Iberians with the falcata, Germanic warriors with their hair in a knot, the Boii's war chariots, Carthage's elephant with its tower. Caesar's legion comes in plate armour under the eagle; rebel gladiators in their arena kit; a village's men with spears and pitchforks; wolves trot, lope and bite. Every blow lands as the game strikes, the fallen lie a while, and a click picks a man as before.
* **Ships and boats in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the Roman merchantman (a round-bellied corbita with its swan's-neck sternpost, a square sail striped in its trading partner's colour, the little foresail raked over the bow and amphorae in the hold), a smaller coaster for the near ports, the fleet's liburnians with their bronze rams and painted eyes, the raiders' own craft (the Ligurians' and Illyrians' light galleys, Carthage's black galleys with a horse's head on the stem, the Gauls' high oak ships with sails of hide) and the fishing boats with their nets. The rowers pull together to the stroke while their oars dip and lift; sailors haul on the sheets; marines stand on the warships' decks and draw their bows in a fight; fishermen cast and haul their nets. Sails fill with the wind and are braced round to it, brailed up at the quay and to fight; hulls rock on the swell and leave a wake; merchant ships moor alongside, warships stern-to their station; a lantern burns at the stern at night, and a ship that is sunk goes down. A click on a hull opens its panel.
* **Aqueducts and reservoirs in 3D (WebGL renderer):** an aqueduct is a Roman arcade, an arch a tile, carrying an open channel lined in pink signinum; you see the water running along the top while it carries water, and silt in it when it is dry. It turns and branches on solid piers, crosses a road on a wide dressed arch with a blank tablet over it, and steps down into a reservoir in a stair of little falls. The reservoir is a castellum aquae: a lined tank on a paved podium with a small distribution house at its back (three outlets behind bronze grilles, three lead pipes out of its back), two water towers at its corners and overflows running into troughs; full of water or empty with a puddle, as the game says. Drag an aqueduct and its ghost shows the pieces it will build.
* **Forts, barracks and the military academy in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the legion fort is a little stone fort of the legions, crenellated walls on an earth bank, tiled towers, an arched gate under LEG II AVG, the headquarters with the eagle and the standards before it (gone out with the men while the fort is deployed); the archer fort is turf and timber, towers on posts, leather tents and straw butts; the cavalry fort is limewashed, with a stable where a horse stands in a stall for each of its troopers and the cavalry's dragon standard. Your soldiers at rest still stand in the yard where you can see and click them. The barracks' racks fill with the shields, pila, helmets and arrows it holds and its horse line with its horses, and a recruit drills at the post while one trains; the academy is a training ground with a painted hall, posts, butts and a riding ring, busy while men train there.
* **The school, the library and the academy in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the school is a master's portico open to the street, boys on benches with their wax tablets round the master in his wicker chair under a striped awning, ARMA VIRVMQVE CANO, the Aeneid's first words that Pompeii's schoolboys scratched on its walls, painted on a whitened board; the library is a marble-columned book hall with cupboards of scrolls, Minerva on her pedestal and herms of its authors; the academy is a garden court with a half-round exedra where the master sits among his pupils, a sundial, cypresses and a pupil declaiming from a platform. Busy while staffed, shut and empty without workers, lanterns lit at night.
* **Gardens, statues and the triumphal arch in 3D (WebGL renderer):** gardens after the excavated gardens of Pompeii, with box hedges, gravel walks, roses, oleander, myrtle, laurel, acanthus and lilies round a marble basin, a sundial, a pool or a vine pergola hung with marble discs; side by side, gardens grow into one garden, and they follow the seasons (roses in May, oleanders in high summer, the vintage in autumn, bare vines in winter). The statues are a herm, a bust or a gilt Victory, the Augustus of Prima Porta, a patron in his toga or a bronze general, and a gilded horseman after Marcus Aurelius or an emperor enthroned, on bases cut in Roman capitals. Left untended, gardens run wild and statues weather green and streaked with a fallen wreath. The gardeners' yard has its shed, pots of seedlings and a gardener clipping box; the triumphal arch, after the Arch of Titus, spans its road with its four-horse chariot on top.
* **The barber, the physician, the baths and the hospital in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the barber's is a shop open to the street, TONSOR over the door, a client on a stool under a linen cloth being shaved while a neighbour waits on the bench and another talks, a bronze mirror and towels inside, boarded up when it has no staff; the physician's is an ochre consulting room under a little pediment, MEDICVS over the door, the doctor taking a patient's pulse by the couch, instruments laid out, a remedy ground in a mortar, coals in a brazier, herbs in pots and the staff of Asclepius with its serpent by the door; the baths have a brick caldarium under a barrel vault, a round room under a cone of a dome, a portico, a palaestra with ball players and a pool with a bronze spout, the furnace, its stoker and firewood behind: with piped water and staff the fire is in and smoke rises (and in a hard frost steam, the snow melted off the warm vaults), with water and no staff the pool lies still and cold, without water it is dry, and the pool freezes in a hard frost; the hospital is a court of wards after the army's hospitals, a bed seen through each door, a corridor of columns round a garden of herbs, the operating hall at the back with the surgeon at work, VALETVDINARIVM over the gate. Their lanterns (and the caldarium's window) light at night while they work.
* **Monuments rising in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the Hall of Justice goes up before your eyes, stage by stage and course by course as its work is done: the plan set out with stakes and cords, the podium's concrete in its shuttering, brick walls climbing inside scaffolds of lashed poles with masons on their top boards, column drums swung up by a treadwheel crane with a man walking in its wheel, the arch over the apse on its timber centering, the roof's trusses one by one, then the tiles, the apse's half dome cast on its frame; finished, a white basilica with a portico of Ionic columns where an advocate pleads, litigants argue and a money changer counts coin. Halted, the crew goes home and the crane stands still; struck by raiders, its scaffolds lie toppled among rubble. The goods the carts bring pile up on site. The work camp is a builders' yard: masons' and carpenters' sheds, the crews' leather tents, the clerk's plans and his groma, a kitchen fire, water jars, ox wagons parked while they are not on the road, braziers at its gate at night, its crew at home or away at the site, its fire cold and its jars dry when it runs short.
* **The senate house and the governor's residences in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the senate house is a tall brick hall on a podium after the Curia Julia, its front stuccoed white and scored like marble, a porch of six Corinthian columns cut CVRIA, bronze doors under S·P·Q·R and a gilt Victory on its gable; in session senators in the toga come up the steps and talk in the porch while a magistrate's lictors wait with the fasces. The governor's house is a Pompeian atrium house (HAVE in the threshold, a porch over the door, the atrium round its pool, a garden behind red-and-white columns, painted walls, a guard at the door); the villa a great peristyle with a playing fountain in a long pool, marble statues and a dining room open on the garden where the governor dines with his guests; the palace stands on a platform, a gate of Corinthian columns cut REGIA up a flight between gilt statues and the governor's standards, a court of Ionic porticoes with two fountains, and the hall behind gilt Corinthian capitals, LEGATVS AVG PR PR on its frieze and a gilt eagle on its gable. Lived in, the lamps are lit at night; with no servants the house is shut up; when a mob makes for it or an enemy comes near, the doors are barred, the household goes in and soldiers line the door. Their pools freeze in a hard frost.
* **The temples, the oracle and the mission post in 3D (WebGL renderer):** a small temple is a Roman podium temple in small (after the Temple of Portunus in Rome and the Maison Carree at Nimes): steps up the front, a porch of four columns, bronze doors, the frieze painted in the god's colours with the dedication in gilt letters, the god's emblems in the pediment and on the gable, and the altar in front of the steps, where the rite was done. Each god is its own: Ceres in the old Tuscan way with a gilt sheaf and terracotta palmettes, sheaves at the altar and a plough; Neptune Ionic in sea blue with a trident between dolphins and an anchor given by sailors; Mercury Corinthian in green and gold with the caduceus and a herm; Mars crimson with a trophy of arms, a crested helmet on the gable and gilded shields on the walls; Venus pale blue and rose with a shell between doves, myrtles and roses. A grand temple is the marble temple of Mars Ultor in a court of porticoes with a big altar. Through the open doors you glimpse the god's statue. At work, the fire burns on the altar and the priest offers at it with his head veiled; in its god's festival month there are garlands, a crowd in wreaths, a flute player and the victim; unstaffed, the doors are shut and the altar cold; and when a god is angry, the smoke over its altars turns black. The oracle is a round shrine of ten Corinthian columns over a spring, vapour rising from the cleft in the rock beside it, bronze tripods burning; the mission post a walled yard with a shrine of Peace, the envoys' lodging and gifts for the villages.
* **The theatre, the amphitheatre, the Great Arena and the hippodrome in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the theatre is a small Roman theatre after Pompeii's: a half circle of stone seats round the orchestra, the decurions in their chairs, a stage in front of a two-storey wall of coloured marble columns with its three doors and statues, an awning on masts; on a play day masked actors declaim and a piper plays while the audience watches. The amphitheatre is Pompeii's in small, its banks held by a wall of blind arches with the double stair up the outside; the Great Arena is the Colosseum in miniature, three storeys of arches framed by columns, the attic with its shields and masts, the governor's box and the trapdoors in the sand. Gladiators fight there (a murmillo against a thraex, a retiarius with his net and trident), a referee watching, and at the Arena a hunter faces a lion. The hippodrome is the Circus Maximus: the spina with its obelisk, the seven eggs and seven dolphins that count the laps, the gilded turning posts, the starting gates, and four chariots of the red, white, green and blue racing round at the gallop. The seats fill with the crowd as far as your city can fill them, citizens in white togas at the front, and the crowd jumps up as the chariots pass.
* **The Great Baths and the Caravanserai in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the Great Baths are the imperial baths in miniature, after Caracalla's and Diocletian's: the open-air swimming pool before a façade of columns and statues, the cold hall under three cross vaults lit by half-round windows, the domed hot room bulging out at the back with its tall windows, an exercise court down each side with its colonnade, the furnaces, the woodstore and the cistern behind. Working, bathers swim and stand in the pool, ball players and weightlifters exercise, the stoker feeds the fire and smoke rises from the flues; in a frost steam pours from the windows and the dome. Without timber the furnaces go dark; without piped water the pools stand empty. The Caravanserai is a great road station: two storeys of rooms round a court, a gallery over a colonnade, a gate tall enough for a wagon, stables with mules and horses at their mangers, an ox wagon unloading at the stores, travellers eating under the vine at the tavern. Both rise stage by stage on their building site, with scaffolds, cranes and centering, and show the raiders' damage when sacked.
* **The training buildings of the shows in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the actor troupe rehearses in masks on a stage of boards before its house, the piper playing and the manager directing, its touring cart in the yard; at the gladiator school a murmillo and a thraex spar in a little practice ring, a retiarius casts his net, recruits strike the posts; the menagerie's lion paces its cage and roars, the bear rises on its hind feet, keepers carry the meat; the chariot stable shows its faction's colour, its horses at their mangers and a team trotting round a turning post. Each shows when a performer is on the road (the cart gone, the leopard's cage open, a team away).
* **Native villages in 3D (WebGL renderer):** the Ligurians live as their hillforts are dug: round and oval huts on footings of dry stone, wattle and daub under cones of thatch with smoke seeping out, and higher up small stone huts whose thatch is roped down with hanging stones; their meeting place a ring round a great hearth under an old oak, a carved statue-stele of their own Lunigiana, a lean-to and a fold of goats and sheep; their plots terraced behind dry stone walls, spelt, barley, millet and beans growing and ripening with the months. The sandbox's villagers build the oval huts with crossed roof timbers of the old Latin hut urns, pale round houses and hipped longhuts round a ring of carved posts. At peace the women grind grain on saddle querns and spin with drop spindles at their doors, carry water on their heads, the men mend hurdles and watch the flock, children play in the dust, the elders talk by the cooking pot and a plot is hoed; angry, the men gather at the fire with their spears while the war horn sounds; attacking, the men are away; trading, hides, fleeces, honey, cheese and timber are laid out for your mission's trader. Each hut's door faces its meeting place and glows with its hearth at night.
* **The Great Sanctuaries, the Pantheon and the Lighthouse in 3D (WebGL renderer):** a Great Sanctuary is a terraced hillside sanctuary after Praeneste, Terracina and Tibur: ramps across a face of polygonal stone, a terrace of arched rooms, a stair up to the summit court with its porticoes and the god's own temple, painted and gilded as the god's temples are, FANVM MARTIS (or the god's own name) cut over the arches; each god's ground on the lowest terrace: Ceres's sacred grove and her mystic basket, Neptune's spring in marble basins with bronze dolphins spouting, Mercury's market stalls and caduceus, Mars's trophies of captured arms and his sacred spears, Venus's roses in myrtle hedges and her doves. The Pantheon is Hadrian's: sixteen granite columns, M AGRIPPA L F COS TERTIVM FECIT on the frieze, the brick drum with its relieving arches, the stepped rings and the dome sheathed in gilt bronze to the oculus. The Lighthouse rises in square, eight-sided and round tiers on a platform of arches over the sea, a winding ramp up its square storey, its fire before a bronze mirror lit at night and smoking by day while its keepers carry the wood up, dark when its store runs out of timber. Each is built before your eyes stage by stage, walls rising course by course inside scaffolds, arches and the dome on their timber centering, treadwheel cranes turning while the work camp's crew is on site and standing still when work is halted; raiders leave rubble and fallen columns; priests, processions and worshippers fill a finished sanctuary, and on its god's festival the feast.
* **Music:** ten original tracks of a few minutes each for building and for the night, festival music, and war drums when raiders attack, all played live by synthesized lyre, pipes and drums.

Press **F1** in the game for the full manual. The rules and numbers are in [docs/GAMEPLAY.md](docs/GAMEPLAY.md).

## Getting started

1. **Roads first.** Walkers only move on roads, and your city must connect to the Imperial road. The gateway with green pennants is the map entrance, where settlers arrive.
2. **Housing plots** (Area, H) beside the roads attract settlers, who pitch tents. Every building with workers needs a road touching one of its edges (any side; a corner does not count), or it gets no workers: a red sign with a crossed-out road floats over any building that has none.
3. **Water:** a Puteus (Well) turns tents into family tents. Later, a Castellum Aquae (Reservoir) pipes water to a Lacus (Fountain) for better homes. With the Housing tool in hand, an outlined blue shows where homes would get water.
4. **Safety:** an Excubitorium (Prefecture: against fire, and as police against thieves and rioters) and a Collegium Fabrum (Engineer's Post: against collapse) must send walkers past every building. Their walkers head for the streets closest to disaster, but each post looks after its own neighbourhood, so spread them out.
5. **Food:** Seges (Wheat Farm) on meadow → Granarium (Granary) → Macellum (Market). Market vendors sell door to door. With a farm in hand the meadow is outlined in green-gold, even under snow.
6. **Grow:** temples, schools, theaters, baths and a Forum (for taxes) let homes move up. Their walkers head for the streets whose homes most need a visit, but each building serves its own neighbourhood.
7. **Click everything.** Every building says what it is doing and what it lacks; walkers, soldiers, raiders, wolves and ships say who they are and what they are up to.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| No music | Browsers only allow sound after a click, tap or key press: the title screen says *Click, tap or press a key to begin*. Otherwise check *Settings* (Music, Music volume, Mute all sounds) and press M. |
| Saves disappeared | Saves live in the browser for that exact file: a different browser, a private window or clearing site data means no saves. Export your cities to files as a backup. |
| An old save will not load | Until version 1.0 a new release may not load older saves, and says so. Start a new city. |
| "Could not save: storage is full" | Delete old slots in *Load game* (each shows its size), or export them to files first. |
| Slow when zoomed far out | Switch off *Ambient effects* and *Weather* in *Settings*. |
| WebGL slow on a laptop | Press **F3**: the readout shows frames a second and the graphics chip the browser uses. If it names the chip in the processor (Intel UHD or Iris, AMD Radeon Graphics) and the laptop has a graphics card, give the browser the card: Windows Settings > System > Display > Graphics, pick the browser, *High performance*, restart the browser. *Settings > Render scale* (Auto, or 75% / 50%) and *Ground* (3D, low quality) make it lighter. |
| Fullscreen | Starting a game goes fullscreen (*Settings > Fullscreen when a game starts*); **Esc** leaves it, the ⛶ button in the top bar goes back in. |
| The game paused by itself | An auto-pause switch in *Settings* (raiders arriving is on at first). The note in the corner says why: click it to look, Space resumes. |
| Night too dark, or rain distracting | Switch off *Day and night* or *Weather* in *Settings* (both are purely visual). |
| In debt and cannot build | Ask Rome for a loan in the Finance advisor (F2): the money comes at once and is repaid monthly with interest. |
| Tax income is falling | Click a home: its tax line says whether a tax collector has registered it, and for how long. A lone Forum's collector can wander off along the Imperial road; a second Forum, or roadblocks at the ends of your blocks, keep collectors on your streets. |
| A building never gets workers | No road touches it. A red sign with a crossed-out road floats over it, and after 8 days a message says where it is. Run a road along any of its edges; a road that only meets a corner does not count. |
| Fountains or baths ran dry | The reservoir feeding them, or an aqueduct linking it to a full one, is gone (raiders, or demolished by mistake). A fountain also needs its workers. Click a reservoir or fountain: its panel says whether it is full. Wells, fountains and reservoirs never burn or wear out. |
| Caesar's legions are coming | The Emperor's favor fell to 10 or less. Raise it before they arrive (his requests, gifts from your savings, troops when he calls for them) and they turn for home; otherwise ready your army. The mission is lost only if the city is overrun. The Imperial advisor (F2) says where they are and what they will do. |
| Ships never come | Sea routes need a river or coast that reaches the map edge, and a staffed Emporium (Trade Dock) standing out over it (two rows on the water). Use land routes on maps without one. If the Emporium's panel says ships are waiting offshore, every Emporium is busy: build another. |
| "Something broke in the city" screen | Click *Copy report* and include it in a bug report. *Download emergency save* keeps your city. |
